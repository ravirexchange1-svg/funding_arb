Scans every asset listed on two or more venues with funding history and ranks short/long pairings by how consistently they paid. Takes a minute or two.

Score is the lower of the net return over the window and the gross return over the last 7 days, so a pair must have paid over both. “Days up” is the share of days with positive funding. There is no liquidity filter, so thin markets are included; check order-book depth before trading. Pairs whose prices differ by more than 5% are dropped as likely different tokens. Paradex is sampled about every 12 hours, so its numbers are approximate. Extended and Aster need the local server. Variational and QFEX publish no history; on localhost they are recorded while the page is open and only appear once enough days have been recorded. Spread now is today’s estimate, shown for comparison only. The same caveats as Backtest apply: price gap, margin, slippage and fees can outweigh funding.

Pick an asset and two venues, or click a row in Compare venues.

Assumes both legs are held for the whole window at a fixed notional, funding is paid on that notional at each settlement, and fees are charged on opening and closing both legs. It ignores the price gap between the two venues (the main risk of this trade), margin, liquidation, borrow costs, slippage and transfer costs. Past funding does not predict future funding.

Funding is shown as an hourly rate; APR = hourly × 24 × 365, simple (positive: longs pay shorts). Venues quote funding over different intervals (1h, 4h, 8h), so each rate is divided by its interval. Aster shows the last settled rate, not a forecast. Pacifica open interest is left out because its API does not state the unit. In Compare, the spread is the gap between the highest and lowest APR for the same asset; “long low / short high” is the pairing that collects it, before fees, borrow and price risk. Search matches part of a name (BTC also finds BTCD and BTCDOM). Aster lists extra quote currencies as, for example, “BTC USDC”. Assets are matched by ticker only, so a very large spread can be two different tokens that share a name; check before trading. Ondo, Phoenix, RISEx, TXFlow and Arcus are not included yet because they have no public browser-readable API I could verify.
